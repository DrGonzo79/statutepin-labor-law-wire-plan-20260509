# GStack Office Hours Diagnostic — Statutepin

## Verdict

Buildable, but only if the wedge is brutally narrow: **multi-state HR generalists who own payroll-adjacent compliance without in-house employment counsel**. Do not start as a generic “labor law intelligence” company. Start as “the alert that prevents next payroll cycle from violating a new wage/leave/scheduling rule.”

## Specific customer

- HR generalists / HR managers at 50–300 employee remote or hybrid companies.
- They hire across 5–10 US states, often because remote talent quietly expanded before process caught up.
- They use BambooHR, Gusto, Rippling, ADP, Paychex, or Justworks, but still manually interpret jurisdiction-specific changes.
- They are accountable for compliance but not expert employment attorneys.

## Painful status quo

- Quarterly law firm newsletters arrive too late for payroll-cycle decisions.
- Government feeds are fragmented, attorney-oriented, and noisy.
- HR creates fragile spreadsheets and Slack reminders for state-by-state policy obligations.
- Legal review is expensive and reactive; HR avoids asking until risk feels material.
- Audit trail is weak: “we saw it in a newsletter” is not a defensible operational record.

## Desperate use case

“Before Friday payroll, tell me whether any wage, sick-time, leave, scheduling, posting, or local ordinance change affects employees in CA, NY, WA, CO, or IL — and give me the exact action checklist.”

That is a hair-on-fire job. It has timing pressure, penalty exposure, and a named owner.

## Narrowest paid wedge

**Payroll-cycle labor law alert pack for five high-change states.**

- Jurisdictions: CA, NY, WA, CO, IL to start; add cities only where customer concentration demands it.
- Scope: wage/hour, sick leave, paid leave, scheduling, required notices/posters, effective-date changes.
- Delivery: Slack + email + dashboard + audit log.
- Human-reviewed summaries with confidence labels.
- $299/month pilot for 5 states; $499/month for 10 states + HRIS employee-location sync.

## Observation / validation plan

1. Interview 20 HR generalists in remote-first companies with 50–300 employees.
2. Ask for last 3 compliance scares, not opinions about software.
3. Run a concierge alert service for 10–15 pilots for 30 days.
4. Measure whether alerts cause concrete actions: policy edit, payroll rule update, employee comms, legal escalation.
5. Price test at $299/month only after one alert produces a real action.
6. Track trust: “Would you forward this to legal/payroll?” is the actual quality bar.

## Future-fit

If the wedge works, Statutepin expands into a compliance operations graph: jurisdictions → employee populations → policy obligations → actions → audit evidence. Future defensibility comes from feed coverage, reviewer QA data, customer-specific obligation mapping, integrations, and accumulated response logs — not from generic AI summarization.

## Killer risks

- False confidence is worse than no product. This must not pretend to provide legal advice.
- Jurisdiction coverage can sprawl into an unprofitable services business.
- Large incumbents can bundle generic alerting; Statutepin needs speed, specificity, and workflow proof.
- HR will churn if alerts are noisy or not tied to concrete actions.
